Jobs in Czechia: The Complete 2026 Guide

Czechia has one of Europe's tightest labour markets — near-full employment, a huge shared-services scene in Prague and Brno, and living costs far below Vienna or Munich just a few hours' drive away.

Reviewed August 2026. Wage and threshold figures are indexed every year — confirm current values on the official Ministry of the Interior pages before applying.

Why work in Czechia?

  • Jobs everywhere. Unemployment has sat near the EU's lowest levels for years — employers compete for qualified staff, including foreigners.
  • A shared-services powerhouse. Prague and Brno host hundreds of global SSC, GBS and IT delivery centres that hire in English.
  • Central and connected. Berlin, Vienna, Warsaw and Munich are all within half a day — with Czech rents a fraction of those cities.
  • Cheap by EU standards. Average salaries are lower than the West, but rent, food and transport cost correspondingly less.

In-demand jobs in Czechia

  • IT & software — Prague and Brno: development, QA, DevOps and data at companies like Red Hat, IBM and hundreds of product firms
  • Shared services & finance — accounting, payroll, HR and customer support hubs hiring English speakers across Prague, Brno and Ostrava
  • Engineering & automotive — Škoda Auto and its suppliers, plus machinery, electronics and aerospace manufacturing
  • Manufacturing & quality — production planners, quality engineers and technicians in the industrial regions
  • Healthcare — doctors and nurses are in chronic shortage (Czech language required)

The average gross wage is about CZK 51,500 per month (roughly €2,050) and the 2026 minimum wage is CZK 22,400 per month. Prague pays 15–25% above the national average; software salaries of CZK 700,000–1,300,000 per year equal roughly €28,000–€52,000.

Work permits & visas

  • EU, EEA and Swiss citizens: free movement — no work permit needed.
  • Employee Card (zaměstnanecká karta): the standard non-EU route. It combines work and residence, but the job must be listed in the Ministry of the Interior's central registry of vacancies open to foreigners.
  • EU Blue Card: for university graduates with a job paying at least about CZK 73,800 gross per month (roughly €2,950 — around 1.5× the average wage) from May 2026.
  • Regulated professions: doctors, nurses, teachers and some engineers must have qualifications officially recognised ('nostrified') first.
  • Official source: Ministry of the Interior of the Czech Republic (mvcr.cz).

How to apply in Czechia

  • Start in English-speaking hubs. IT and shared services run entire interview processes in English in Prague and Brno.
  • Check the vacancy registry. For an Employee Card the offer must come from a registered vacancy — ask the employer before you invest time.
  • Nostrification takes time. If your profession is regulated, start degree recognition early; it can take months.
  • Brno is the insider pick. Lower rents than Prague, a big tech scene (Red Hat's largest engineering site) and a large expat community.

FAQ — jobs in Czechia

  • What is the Czech Employee Card?
    Czechia's combined work and residence permit for non-EU nationals. The job must be posted in the Ministry of the Interior's central registry of vacancies available to foreigners, and the card ties you to that employer and role until you apply for a change.
  • How much do jobs in Czechia pay?
    The average gross wage is about CZK 51,500 per month (≈€2,050). Software developers in Prague typically earn CZK 700,000–1,300,000 per year (≈€28,000–€52,000); the 2026 minimum wage is CZK 22,400 per month.
  • Is English enough to work in Czechia?
    In IT, shared services and multinational engineering — yes, especially in Prague and Brno. Healthcare, education and most local companies require Czech.
  • Which cities should I target?
    Prague (the biggest market, highest pay), Brno (tech and SSCs), Ostrava and Plzeň (engineering and manufacturing), plus university cities like Olomouc.
  • Do I need a university degree for a Czech work permit?
    Not for the Employee Card — but the job must be a registered vacancy and regulated professions need recognised qualifications. The EU Blue Card does require a completed university degree plus the salary threshold.
