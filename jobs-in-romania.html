Jobs in Romania: The Complete 2026 Guide

Romania is the EU's fastest-growing large job market — a booming IT scene that produced UiPath and Bitdefender, big automotive and outsourcing industries, and a work-permit quota that lets tens of thousands of non-EU workers in every year.

Reviewed August 2026. Quota and wage figures change annually — confirm on the official General Inspectorate for Immigration pages before applying.

Why work in Romania?

  • Rising fast. Salaries have grown at some of the EU's highest rates for a decade, while living costs stay among the lowest.
  • A real tech hub. Bucharest and Cluj-Napoca host engineering centres for Amazon, Microsoft, Oracle and a deep bench of local product companies.
  • Clear non-EU route. The government approves a work-permit quota every year (90,000 for 2026) aimed at shortages in construction, manufacturing, logistics and services.
  • English-friendly business culture. Multinationals and IT companies interview and work in English; Romania also has a digital nomad visa.

In-demand jobs in Romania

  • IT & software — Bucharest, Cluj-Napoca, Iași and Timișoara: development, QA, DevOps, data — the best-paid sector in the country
  • Engineering & automotive — Dacia/Renault, Continental, Bosch and suppliers: R&D, production, quality
  • Construction — the largest allocation of the annual non-EU work-permit quota
  • BPO & customer support — Bucharest, Cluj and Brașov serve global clients in many languages
  • Healthcare — doctors and nurses remain in shortage (Romanian language required)

The average gross wage is about RON 9,500 per month (≈€1,880); the minimum wage rises to RON 4,325 gross per month from July 2026. IT is the best-paid sector with an average net of around RON 8,000 (≈€1,600) per month — senior developers in Bucharest and Cluj earn well above that, and many work on more favourable B2B contracts.

Work permits & visas

  • EU, EEA and Swiss citizens: free movement — no permit needed.
  • Non-EU workers: the government sets an annual quota — 90,000 permits for 2026 (down from 100,000 in 2025). The employer obtains a work authorisation from the General Inspectorate for Immigration (IGI), then the worker applies for a long-stay visa; quota-based applicants no longer need a visa interview in 2026.
  • Digital nomad visa: available for foreign remote employees and freelancers with income of roughly three times the average Romanian net salary (about €3,300+ per month).
  • Regulated professions: healthcare and some technical roles require recognised qualifications and Romanian language proof.
  • Official source: General Inspectorate for Immigration (igi.gov.ro).

How to apply in Romania

  • Interview in English. IT, BPO and multinational engineering teams hire in English end-to-end.
  • Understand B2B contracting. Many IT roles are offered via PFA/SRL (freelance-style) contracts with different tax and benefits — compare net income carefully.
  • Quota fills up. Work authorisations are first-come-first-served within the annual quota — employers apply early in the year.
  • Cluj vs Bucharest. Bucharest has the most openings and highest pay; Cluj-Napoca offers a strong tech scene with a calmer lifestyle.

FAQ — jobs in Romania

  • How many work permits does Romania issue?
    The government approved a quota of 90,000 non-EU work permits for 2026, down from 100,000 in 2025. Most go to construction, manufacturing, logistics and services, and quota-based workers no longer attend a visa interview.
  • How much do jobs in Romania pay?
    The average gross wage is about RON 9,500/month (≈€1,880). IT leads the market with an average net of roughly RON 8,000/month (≈€1,600); senior developers earn much more. The gross minimum wage becomes RON 4,325/month in July 2026.
  • Is English enough to work in Romania?
    In IT, BPO and multinational companies — yes. Healthcare, public-sector and most small local employers require Romanian.
  • Which cities should I target?
    Bucharest (largest market, highest salaries), Cluj-Napoca (the top tech hub), Timișoara and Iași (automotive and IT), Brașov and Sibiu (growing industrial and support centres).
  • Does Romania have a digital nomad visa?
    Yes — remote employees and freelancers can qualify with a monthly income of roughly three times the average net salary (around €3,300 or more), plus standard documentation.
