Work on a Student Visa — and Stay After Graduation (2026 Guide)

Reviewed by WintWorks Editorial Team · Last updated September 11, 2026 · 11 min read

A student visa is not just a study permit — in most popular destinations it comes with part-time work rights during your degree and a post-study work permit after it. Used well, that combination is one of the most reliable immigration routes into the US and European job markets. This guide explains the work-hour limits while you study and the post-graduation visas in the UK, Germany, the Netherlands, Ireland, France, Spain and the USA.

Rule zero: the condition printed on your visa or residence permit always wins. Exceeding your work limit is a status violation that can sink both your degree and any later work-visa application.

UK: the Graduate Route is shrinking — time your application

  • Graduates of UK universities can stay without sponsorship and work full time in most roles.
  • Apply on or before 31 December 2026 → 2 years. Apply from 1 January 2027 → 18 months. PhD graduates keep 3 years either way.
  • There is no minimum salary on the Graduate Route itself — but it is a bridge, not a settlement route. Your real target is switching to a Skilled Worker visa (sponsor + salary threshold), and sponsors prefer candidates already in the UK.
  • Act early: shorter runway means employers must decide faster, so start applying for sponsored roles before you graduate.

Germany: 18 unrestricted months to convert your degree into a job

  • After graduating from a German university you can switch to an 18-month residence permit to seek work (§20 Residence Act).
  • During those months you may work any job, full time — ideal for bridging income while you land a qualified position.
  • Once you find qualified employment, switch to an EU Blue Card or skilled-worker permit; time on the job-seeker permit counts toward settlement.
  • Didn't make it in 18 months? A viable freelance business can qualify for the self-employment permit (§21) — see the freelancing guide.

Netherlands, Ireland, France, Spain — one year to launch

  • Netherlands: the orientation year (zoekjaar hoogopgeleiden) gives 12 months of free labour-market access; you can apply up to 3 years after graduating. Graduates who then qualify for the Highly Skilled Migrant scheme benefit from a reduced salary threshold.
  • Ireland: Stamp 1G — 12 months after an honours bachelor, 24 months after a master's or PhD. Move to a Critical Skills Employment Permit (tech, engineering, healthcare, finance) for a long-term path.
  • France: master's graduates can request the APS — a 12-month permit to look for work or start a business; then a standard work permit or Talent Passport.
  • Spain: graduates of Spanish universities can get a 12-month job-search residence, and Spain's digital-nomad route also accepts the self-employed — see the freelancing guide.

USA: OPT and STEM OPT — the 36-month plan

  • OPT: 12 months of post-completion work authorisation for F-1 students, in a job related to your major. Apply up to 90 days before and no later than 60 days after your programme end date — late filings are rejected.
  • STEM OPT: +24 months if your degree is on the STEM list, your employer is E-Verify enrolled and you file a Form I-983 training plan. Total: up to 36 months.
  • Watch the unemployment clock: max 90 days during OPT, 150 days total including STEM OPT.
  • The long-term goal is an H-1B (lottery, employer-sponsored, with cap-gap protection while your OPT remains valid) or alternatives such as O-1 or an intra-company transfer.

Your graduate-to-job-offer checklist

  1. Mark the application windows now. Graduate Route by 31 Dec 2026 for 2 years; OPT 90 days before your programme ends; Dutch orientation year within 3 years of graduating.
  2. Start applying 4–6 months before graduation. Sponsorship processes (UK Skilled Worker, US H-1B) take longer than your visa runway suggests.
  3. Target employers who already sponsor. Use each country guide on this site to shortlist markets where your occupation is in demand.
  4. Make your CV pass both machines and humans — see the ATS resume guide, then build it in the free CV Builder.
  5. Never pay an agent for a guaranteed job or visa. Government fees are paid through official channels only — learn how to verify offers.

FAQ — student visa work & post-study visas

  • How many hours can I work on a student visa in the UK?
    On a UK Student visa, degree-level students can work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during official breaks. You cannot be self-employed or work as a professional sportsperson or entertainer. Always check the work condition printed on your BRP/eVisa.
  • How long is the UK Graduate Route visa now?
    Applications made on or before 31 December 2026 receive 2 years (3 years for PhD graduates). From 1 January 2027 the duration for bachelor's and master's graduates drops to 18 months; PhD graduates keep 3 years. No job offer or sponsorship is required and you can work full time in most roles while you switch to a Skilled Worker visa.
  • How long can I stay in Germany after graduation to find a job?
    Graduates of German universities can get an 18-month residence permit to seek work (Section 20 of the Residence Act). During those 18 months you may work unrestricted — any job, full time — until you find qualified employment, then switch to an EU Blue Card or skilled-worker permit.
  • What is OPT and STEM OPT in the USA?
    Optional Practical Training (OPT) gives F-1 students 12 months of work authorisation after graduation in a job related to their degree. STEM degree graduates can extend by 24 more months with an E-Verify employer and a Form I-983 training plan — up to 36 months total. Apply up to 90 days before and no later than 60 days after your programme end date.
  • Can I work while I am still studying?
    Yes, with limits that depend on the country: UK 20 hours/week in term time; Germany 140 full days or 280 half days per year; USA on-campus 20 hours/week (plus CPT for off-campus internships); EU/EEA citizens face no limits anywhere in the EU. Ireland allows casual work on Stamp 2 (20 h/week in term, 40 h in holidays).
