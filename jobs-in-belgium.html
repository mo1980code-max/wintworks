Jobs in Belgium: The Complete 2026 Guide

Belgium packs Western-European salaries into a small, highly international market — Brussels hosts the EU institutions and NATO, Antwerp runs Europe's largest port, and the Single Permit bundles work and residence rights into one application.

Reviewed August 2026. Permit rules and thresholds are indexed almost every year — always confirm on the official sources linked below before you apply.

Why work in Belgium?

  • The EU capital. Brussels is home to the European Commission, Parliament, NATO and thousands of law firms, consultancies and NGOs that hire internationally.
  • One clear permit. Since 2019 the Single Permit combines work authorisation and residence in a single employer-filed application.
  • Strong industry. Pharmaceuticals and chemicals (a genuine 'pharma valley'), logistics through the Port of Antwerp-Bruges, engineering and healthcare.
  • Solid benefits. A 13th-month salary is standard in most collective agreements, plus strong worker protections and social security.

In-demand jobs in Belgium

  • Software & IT — Brussels, Ghent and Leuven: developers, data and cybersecurity at banks, scale-ups and the EU bubble
  • Pharma & biotech — the Walloon pharma cluster and Groot-Bijgaarden/Olen sites: R&D, QA/QC, production
  • Logistics & port operations — Antwerp-Bruges: supply chain, customs, warehouse and engineering roles
  • Healthcare & nursing — hospitals and care homes face structural shortages
  • Shared services & finance — Brussels and Ghent host many international SSCs hiring English speakers

The national average is about €3,890 gross per month and the 2026 minimum wage is roughly €2,150 gross per month; most white-collar jobs also pay a 13th month and holiday money on top. Brussels salaries run slightly above the national average.

Work permits & visas

  • EU, EEA and Swiss citizens: free movement — register with the local commune after arrival.
  • Single Permit (highly skilled): for non-EU workers; the employer files one application covering both work and residence. Decision typically within about 4 months.
  • Regional salary thresholds (2026, indicative): roughly €44,000–€53,000 gross per year for highly skilled roles depending on the region (Brussels, Flanders, Wallonia), with lower thresholds for workers under 30.
  • EU Blue Card: available at a higher salary threshold (about €55,000–€69,000 gross per year depending on region) plus a recognised higher-education degree.
  • Official sources: workinbelgium.be and emploi.belgique.be (Federal Public Service Employment).

How to apply in Belgium

  • English works in the bubble. Tech, pharma R&D, EU institutions and SSCs hire in English; most local companies expect French or Dutch at B1–B2.
  • Brussels is bilingual. French and Dutch coexist — for public-facing roles one of the two is normally required.
  • Compare total compensation. 13th month, holiday pay and meal vouchers can add 10–15% to the base salary.
  • Let the employer file. You cannot apply for a Single Permit yourself — target employers experienced in hiring non-EU staff.

FAQ — jobs in Belgium

  • What is the Belgian Single Permit?
    Belgium's combined work and residence permit for non-EU workers, in force since 2019. Your employer files a single application with the competent region (Flanders, Wallonia, Brussels or the German-speaking Community) and you usually get a decision within about four months.
  • How much do jobs in Belgium pay?
    The average gross salary is about €3,890 per month. Indicative annual ranges: software developers €45,000–€70,000, engineers €42,000–€62,000, nurses €40,000–€55,000. The 2026 minimum wage is roughly €2,150 gross per month and a 13th month is standard.
  • Is English enough to work in Belgium?
    Yes in tech, pharma R&D, shared services, the EU institutions and many multinationals. For most local companies and public-sector roles you need French or Dutch — in Brussels often both at a basic level.
  • Which cities should I target?
    Brussels (EU institutions, finance, tech, everything international), Antwerp (port, logistics, pharma and diamonds), Ghent and Leuven (tech and research), Liège and Charleroi (industry and logistics).
  • Can I move to Belgium first, then find a job?
    Not in general — Belgium has no ordinary non-EU jobseeker visa, and the Single Permit is filed by an employer. Limited 'orientation year' rights exist for some former highly skilled workers and researchers after their contract ends.
