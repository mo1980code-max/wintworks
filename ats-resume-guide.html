ATS Resume Guide 2026: Make Your CV Pass Automated Screening

Reviewed by WintWorks Editorial Team · Last updated September 11, 2026 · 8 min read

Before a recruiter ever sees your name, software has usually already read your CV. An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) converts your document into a structured profile, searches it for the skills the employer listed, and shows the recruiter a ranked list. If the system mis-parses your CV — or never finds the words it is looking for — your application can sink without anyone deciding anything. This guide shows you exactly how to format and word a CV that survives that first automated pass.

What an ATS actually does with your CV

  1. Parses the document into fields: name, contact details, employer, job title, dates, education, skills.
  2. Profiles you in its database and may enrich the record (LinkedIn, assessments).
  3. Ranks or filters candidates against the job's requirements — by keyword match, years of experience, location or screening questions.

Common systems you will meet: Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, SAP SuccessFactors, Oracle Taleo, SmartRecruiters and UKG. They all parse documents, so the same formatting rules apply everywhere: simple, text-based and predictable.

Formatting rules that decide whether your CV is readable

  • One single column. Two-column and side-bar layouts are the number one parsing killer.
  • Standard section headings — Work Experience, Education, Skills. Creative headings ("My Journey") confuse the parser.
  • No tables, text boxes, columns or shapes. Put each fact on its own line as plain text.
  • Nothing important in headers/footers. Many systems skip them — keep your name and phone in the body.
  • No photos, icons, logos, skill bars or charts. US employers often require photo-free CVs; in the UK, Ireland and the Netherlands photos are discouraged anyway.
  • Standard fonts (Arial, Calibri, Georgia) at 10.5–12 pt. No decorative fonts.
  • Consistent dates — pick MM/YYYY and use it everywhere.
  • File format: .docx is the safest all-rounder; PDF is fine when exported as real text (never a scanned image). If the ad specifies one, obey it.
  • Clean filename: Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf — not cv_final_v7.pdf.

Keywords: how the machine decides you "match"

Ranking is mostly about whether your CV contains the same words the job ad uses. Do this:

  • Mirror the ad's exact phrasing. If it says "stakeholder management", do not write only "liaised with partners".
  • Spell acronyms both ways once: "Search Engine Optimisation (SEO)". Parsers match either form, humans appreciate the clarity.
  • Put hard skills in the Skills section and prove them in bullets. A skill with context outranks a bare keyword.
  • Match the job title where honest. If the ad says "Data Analyst" and your real title was "Reporting Specialist", write: Reporting Specialist (Data Analyst).
  • Never keyword-stuff or use white hidden text. Recruiters see it, and it reads as cheating.

Section-by-section: what good looks like

Contact

Name, city+country, phone, email, LinkedIn — plain text, no icons. Email from a professional address.

Summary

2–3 lines: role + years + two quantified wins, echoing the ad's top requirements.

Experience

Reverse-chronological. Company · Title · MM/YYYY–MM/YYYY · 3–5 bullets with numbers.

Skills

8–15 hard skills as a simple comma list — tools, languages, certifications. No graphs.

Write experience bullets in the X-Y-Z pattern: "Achieved [X], as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]." Example: "Cut invoice processing time 34% (from 11 to 7 days) by automating approval routing in SAP." Start with strong verbs — led, built, reduced, negotiated, migrated — and skip "responsible for".

The 60-second ATS self-test (free)

  1. Open your CV file, Select All → Copy.
  2. Paste into Notepad (or any plain-text editor).
  3. Check: Are your name, phone and email at the top? Are the sections in order? Is any text missing or merged?

If the plain text version is intact, an ATS can read your CV. If it is broken, simplify the layout until it pastes cleanly — that matters more than any paid scanner score.

Mistakes that quietly kill applications

  • Designer templates with sidebars, columns and skill bars (pretty for humans, unreadable for software)
  • Contact details only in the header/footer — the parser never sees them
  • PDFs exported as images, or scans of a printed CV
  • One generic CV for every application — no keyword match with the specific ad
  • Photos and personal data (age, marital status) on US/UK/NL applications
  • Weeks of "gap" created by inconsistent date formats the parser can't align

Country note for European applications

  • UK, Ireland, Netherlands, Nordics: no photo, no age, no marital status — it also protects you from bias.
  • Germany, Austria, France: photo is optional and increasingly skipped; ATS parsing is identical everywhere.
  • Europass: acceptable in public-sector and academic contexts, but its dense template parses poorly in many commercial systems — a clean standard layout is safer for companies.

FAQ — ATS resumes

  • What is an ATS in job applications?
    An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is the software employers use to collect, parse and rank job applications. Common systems include Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, SAP SuccessFactors, Oracle Taleo and SmartRecruiters. The ATS converts your CV into a structured profile, so a parsing-friendly format matters as much as the content.
  • Should I send my CV as PDF or Word for ATS?
    Both are accepted by modern systems, but .docx is the safest choice for older ATS platforms, and PDF is safe if exported as real text (never an image scan). If the job ad specifies a format, follow it exactly.
  • Do ATS systems automatically reject my CV?
    Rarely by itself. Most systems parse and rank applications and help recruiters filter; a human usually makes the final rejection. But a badly parsed CV can hide your real experience from the recruiter, which effectively ends your application.
  • How do I test if my CV is ATS-friendly?
    Copy your CV and paste it into a plain text editor. If the sections appear in the right order with all contact details intact, an ATS can read it. If text is missing, jumbled or merged into one block, simplify the layout.
  • Should I use an ATS resume scanner tool?
    They can help spot missing keywords, but treat their scores as rough hints, not gospel. The reliable method is to read the job ad yourself and mirror its exact skill words in your Skills section and achievement bullets.
