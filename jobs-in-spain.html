Jobs in Spain: The Complete 2026 Guide

Spain has become one of Europe's favourite destinations for international professionals — great weather, a lower cost of living, and fast-growing tech scenes in Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia and Málaga. And with the digital nomad visa, remote workers can make Spain home without a local employer.

Why work in Spain?

  • Digital nomad visa. Work remotely for a foreign company while living in Spain.
  • Growing tech hubs. Barcelona and Madrid host hundreds of startups and scale-ups that operate in English.
  • Cost of living. Significantly cheaper than London, Amsterdam or Munich — your salary goes further.
  • Lifestyle. Mediterranean pace of life, excellent healthcare and fast transport links.

In-demand jobs in Spain

  • Software & tech — the strongest English-language sector: devs, QA, DevOps
  • Customer support — huge multilingual support sector in Barcelona (often English-only)
  • Design & creative — UI/UX, brand, illustration in startup scenes
  • Sales & account management — international SaaS sales offices
  • Hospitality & tourism — seasonal and permanent roles in English for hotels and resorts

Work permits & visas

  • EU/EEA citizens: free movement — register for an NIE after arrival.
  • Work visa (sponsored): employer applies; longer processing than Northern European routes.
  • Digital nomad visa: for remote workers and freelancers earning above a minimum monthly income.
  • Self-employment (autónomo): freelancers can register and work legally after getting the right permit.

How to apply in Spain

  • Target international companies — they are the ones hiring in English and sponsoring.
  • Mention availability to relocate and your permit situation in the first message.
  • Spanish A2–B1 helps enormously for mixed teams; C1 is needed for local companies.
  • Be patient — Spanish hiring processes are slower than Northern Europe; follow up politely.

FAQ — jobs in Spain

  • Can I work in Spain with only English?
    In tech, startups and customer support — yes, especially in Barcelona and Madrid, plus many fully-remote positions. For local companies outside tech, Spanish is usually required.
  • What is Spain's digital nomad visa?
    Spain's remote-work visa lets non-EU remote employees and freelancers live in Spain while working for foreign companies. You need a stable income above a minimum threshold and health cover.
  • How much do jobs in Spain pay?
    Lower than Northern Europe but with a much lower cost of living: software developers €35,000–€60,000, customer support €22,000–€32,000, English-speaking roles often pay more than local market rates.
  • Which cities should I target?
    Madrid (corporate, finance), Barcelona (tech, startups, design), Valencia (fast-growing hub), Málaga (tech and remote-first companies) and the Canary Islands (many remote-friendly employers).
  • Do Spanish employers sponsor visas?
    Yes — companies can sponsor a work visa for qualified roles, but it takes longer than in the Netherlands or Germany. Remote-first companies hiring abroad often prefer contractors or the digital nomad route.
